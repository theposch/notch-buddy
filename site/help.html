Help

Everything you were going to ask Claude anyway.

What does Notch Buddy do?

It lives in your Mac's notch and watches Claude Code, so you can stop staring at a terminal like it owes you money. Open the notch to see every session: which ones are working, which are done and which need you.

Allow or deny a step right there, check how much usage you've burned through, and Clawd keeps you company while an AI does your job.

How do I set it up?

Download it, unzip it, drag Notch Buddy into Applications. Open it and click Connect to Claude Code. That's it. That's the onboarding.

Connecting adds Notch Buddy to Claude Code's settings file, so Claude Code tells it what's happening. A backup of your settings is kept, and Disconnect in Settings puts them back. Commitment issues, respected.

Why does macOS warn me when I open it?

Notch Buddy isn't signed by Apple yet. That costs money, and this is free. The first time you open it, macOS says it can't check the app. Go to System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll to Security and click Open Anyway. After that it opens normally. Living on the edge.

On macOS 14 you can also right-click the app (or Control-click it), choose Open, then Open again.

Does it work with other coding agents?

No. Just Claude Code, in the Terminal and in the Claude desktop app. He's loyal.

What if my Mac has no notch?

It draws a smaller one at the top of your screen. Everybody gets a notch.

Does anything leave my Mac?

No. No account, no server, no analytics, no “we value your privacy” banner. Claude Code tells Notch Buddy what's happening on your own Mac, and it stays there.

One exception, and you have to switch it on yourself: reading usage from your Claude login. Then your login goes to Anthropic, and only Anthropic, to ask how much you've used. Which is a lot. We both know.

How does it know my usage?

Claude Code shares it while it runs in the Terminal. To see it without the Terminal, go to Settings > Usage and accounts and switch on reading a login that's already on your Mac: Claude Code's, or the Claude desktop app's.

That isn't an official Anthropic feature, so it might stop working one day. If it does, the Terminal still has your back.

Why does it ask for access to my Keychain?

Only if you switch on reading usage from your login, because that's where macOS keeps it. Click Allow usage in the notch, then Always Allow in the macOS prompt.

Since the app isn't signed by Apple yet, macOS asks again after every update. Yes, every time. No, we can't fix that for free.

I have more than one Claude account.

Of course you do. Each account gets its own name and colour, and every session shows which one it's on. In Settings > Usage and accounts you can rename them, hide one, and choose whose usage the closed notch shows. Your work account never has to know.

Can I choose which messages I see?

Yes. In Settings > Messages, start from a preset or switch each moment on or off, like failed tests, pushes or new pull requests. Curate them like your personal brand.

Can I choose what a session row shows?

Yes. In Settings > Sessions, put each detail on the row, on hover, or nowhere: model, account, branch, tests, pull request, cost and more. Or pick a preset: Minimal, Balanced, Everything. Minimalists and hoarders both welcome.

How do I update?

When a new version is out, an Update button appears in the notch and in Settings > General. One click downloads it, checks it, installs it and restarts. Nothing is downloaded until you click.

On version 0.1, download the new version by hand once. After that the button does it.

Is it free?

Yes. Free and open source under the MIT License. No pricing page. No “book a demo.”

Something broken? Tell us on GitHub. Screenshots help. Vibes don’t.
